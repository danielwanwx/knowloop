# Evidence behind the learning loop

Research checked: 2026-09-07. This is a focused review of eight primary studies, not a systematic review or a clinical validation of this coach. Publication dates below are the studies' dates, not search-engine crawl dates. Findings support design choices; they do not establish that an AI skill can guarantee complete knowledge, accurate grading, or an interview offer.

## What the evidence supports

| Evidence and source | Finding and boundary | Coach design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roediger & Karpicke, March 2006, [Test-Enhanced Learning](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/) | In two prose-learning experiments, repeated study helped immediate recall, while retrieval practice improved delayed recall. Confidence after rereading did not reliably reflect delayed performance. These were bounded reading/recall tasks, not engineering interviews. | After a manageable chunk, hide the source and request an independent explanation or solution. Record observed performance separately from confidence. Reading completion is not mastery evidence. |
| Butler & Roediger, April 2008, [Feedback enhances the positive effects and reduces the negative effects of multiple-choice testing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/MC.36.3.604) | Immediate and delayed corrective feedback improved later recall and reduced intrusion of wrong alternatives relative to no feedback. This does not identify one universally optimal feedback delay or justify deliberately teaching misinformation. | Correct the specific misconception after a genuine attempt. Explain why it fails, then require a retry. In Mock, defer teaching until the agreed debrief; do not leave errors uncorrected indefinitely. |
| Cepeda et al., November 2008, [Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention](https://laplab.ucsd.edu/articles/Cepeda%20et%20al%202008_psychsci.pdf) | More than 1,350 participants learned facts; useful spacing depended on how long knowledge needed to last. Increasing the gap first helped and then hurt at a given retention interval. This fact-learning study does not validate a universal sequence of review days for every skill. | Set review dates around the learner's retention horizon, performance, and available time. Treat any default schedule as an adjustable heuristic, never as an exact “Ebbinghaus algorithm.” |
| Renkl, Atkinson & Große, January 2004, [How Fading Worked Solution Steps Works](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:TRUC.0000021815.74806.f6) | Two experiments investigated progressively removing worked steps. The reported advantage involved fewer unproductive learning events; the study did not establish that fading automatically caused more productive self-explanation. Findings concern structured cognitive tasks. | When a novice lacks a prerequisite, provide a small worked example, then a completion problem, then independent work. Fade help based on the answer, not a fixed staircase. Do not keep demonstrating after the learner can solve independently. |
| Chi et al., 1994, [Eliciting Self-Explanations Improves Understanding](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chideleeuwchiulavancher_2.pdf) | Prompting self-explanation during study of an expository biology text improved understanding. This supports constructive explanation, not the proposition that speaking more or producing a polished paraphrase proves understanding. | Ask for the causal link, assumption, or missing step: “Why does that follow?” Then check the explanation against reliable material. English fluency and technical correctness need separate assessments. |
| Taylor & Rohrer, September 2010, [The Effect of Interleaving Practice](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1760/) | With spacing controlled, children practicing four kinds of mathematics problems performed worse during interleaved practice but better on a next-day test. Error analysis implicated choosing the appropriate procedure. This does not show that randomly switching between unrelated subjects always helps. | Mix related alternatives when the learner has enough foundation to distinguish them: heap versus sorting, window versus prefix sums, consistency choices. Ask which method applies and why before execution. Allow focused repair when prerequisites fail. |
| Butler, September 2010, [Repeated testing produces superior transfer of learning relative to repeated studying](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/repeated-testing-produces-superior-transfer-of-learning-relative-/) | Four experiments found benefits on delayed inference questions, including changed knowledge domains. Transfer was measured using particular prose passages and questions, not arbitrary workplace performance. | Include unseen applications with changed conditions. Record what actually transferred: a successful variation is evidence for that capability, not certification of an entire domain. |
| van Eersel et al., December 2016, [The Testing Effect and Far Transfer: The Role of Exposure to Key Information](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5183614/) | A replication found a transfer benefit versus rereading, but the advantage became substantially smaller when rereading also provided focused key information. Retrieval was not the only plausible contributor. | Combine retrieval with clear, targeted teaching and explicit application. Do not market endless questioning as sufficient to create deep understanding. Keep corrections and source quality as first-class parts of the loop. |

## Proposed loop: engineering decisions, not scientific thresholds

The following is an implementation synthesis of the studies above. The precise stages, stop rules, and dates are product choices to be evaluated with real learners.

1. **Define a bounded target.** Turn “learn databases” into something observable, such as explaining an isolation anomaly and choosing a mitigation. Keep a short coverage map of essential subskills and prerequisites. Ask only for information that changes the next activity.
2. **Diagnose without teaching the answer.** Start with one accessible question, short code task, prediction, or explanation. A quick check selects the starting point; speed is not a general proxy for understanding. Allow “I do not know.”
3. **Teach only what the evidence shows is missing.** For a beginner, use one small example and ask them to complete the next step. For an experienced learner, skip elementary exposition. A failed advanced probe may call for prerequisite repair rather than another harder question.
4. **Require independent production.** Close or hide the source. Ask the learner to explain, implement, calculate, draw, or decide. A source-assisted answer is useful practice but must retain that assistance label.
5. **Probe one layer deeper.** Choose a question from the answer: mechanism, justification, counterexample, failure mode, alternative, scale, or ownership. Do not mechanically ask every category or keep escalating beyond the target's relevance.
6. **Correct and retry.** Name the precise error, provide the minimum explanation needed, and request a new attempt. Same-session success after instruction remains repaired performance, pending later independent evidence.
7. **Retest after a delay and with variation.** Separate “still remembers it” from “can apply it under changed conditions.” Increase difficulty or spacing when evidence supports it; shorten the loop after failure. Save the actual answer, help received, assessment, unresolved targets, and next action.

For vocabulary or syntax, short recall and use-in-context checks may suffice. Algorithms need executable reasoning and edge cases. System design needs requirements, causal mechanisms, trade-offs, and failure analysis. Behavioral and resume practice needs truthful concrete evidence, ownership, reflection, and flexible retelling; it has no single textbook answer. These domain adaptations are design inferences, not separately established treatment effects in the cited experiments.

## Scheduling and completion boundaries

Use a near-term follow-up after initial learning, then longer gaps after successful independent retrieval. A possible bootstrap is next session, then several days, then a week or more; these are operational defaults, not validated universal intervals. Schedule around the intended interview or use date. A date in storage is a review queue entry, not a promise of a background reminder. Review due items when the user resumes unless an actual notification mechanism is separately configured.

Maintain a few active targets instead of endlessly expanding the queue. If reviews exceed the session budget, prioritize foundational failures and near-term relevance, explicitly defer others, and reduce new material. Avoid repeating an already-correct answer many times merely to fill a quota.

Use bounded language: “independent today,” “retained after this delay,” and “transferred to this new condition.” Do not turn one answer, a quiz percentage, the passage of time, or fluent speech into “fully mastered.” Completion for a stated scope requires evidence across its relevant subskills; untested areas remain unknown. If the model cannot verify a claim or received an incomplete transcript, mark the assessment uncertain and verify or re-ask before changing the learner's standing.

The full AI-mediated workflow still needs real-session evaluation: whether questions diagnose the intended capability, feedback is technically correct, saved evidence is faithful, and delayed performance improves. The cited studies justify trying these components; they do not validate this implementation by themselves.
